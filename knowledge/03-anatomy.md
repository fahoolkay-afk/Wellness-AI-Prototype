**Clinical notes**
- Scalenes are accessory inspiratory muscles. Hypertonic scalenes and SCM go with upper-chest breathing pattern disorder (see §9). [Stone][PTI-Resp]
- Suboccipital tension → cervicogenic headache. The greater occipital nerve (C2) pierces semispinalis capitis and trapezius about 2–3 cm lateral to the EOP. [general knowledge]
- The PTI digestive manual flags the **vagus nerve** coursing deep in the neck, and suggests neck tension may disturb digestion and respiration. Treat this as a hypothesis, not established fact. [PTI-Dig]

## 7. Neurological screening: dermatome, myotome, reflex

From the files: dermatomes overlap "like roof tiles", so loss of a single root is hard to demonstrate with touch. **Test pain/temperature (pinprick), because those dermatomes are narrower.** Landmarks: **nipple line = T4/T5** and **groin = L1/L2**. **C1 has no cutaneous dermatome.** [Kahle] A dermatome is the skin area supplied by one spinal nerve's sensory root. ## 8. Viscerosomatic referral

Background from the files: the ANS splits into sympathetic ("fight or flight") and parasympathetic ("rest and digest"). Slow, unmyelinated nociceptors carry deep aching pain from muscles, joints and organs. [PTI-Neuro] The PTI digestive manual says GI dysfunction can present as shoulder or back pain, and that **any change in normal GI function warrants referral**. [PTI-Dig] Renal colic gives excruciating mid–low back pain radiating to the abdomen, with urinary frequency, haematuria and nausea. [PTI-Urin]

## 9. Vascular, visceral and system cautions

### Vascular endangerment sites [general knowledge]

| Site | Structures | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| Vertebral artery (suboccipital, C1–C2 TP/atlas groove; transverse foramina C6–C1) | Vertebral a., suboccipital n. | High-velocity end-range rotation/extension carries cervical artery dissection risk. | Posterior triangle / interscalene | Brachial plexus, subclavian a./v., external jugular | Gentle scalene work; stop if distal paresthesia |
| Axilla | Axillary a./v., brachial plexus cords, axillary nodes | Light, broad contact only |
| Cubital fossa | Brachial a., median n. | Avoid deep pressure |
| Femoral triangle (inguinal lig., sartorius, adductor longus) | Femoral n./a./v. (lateral→medial "NAVL"), inguinal nodes | Palpate the pulse first and work lateral to it. Caution with iliopsoas release. Hernia may present here |

### System-specific cautions (from files)
- **Abdominal work.** Suspected appendicitis: no pressure, immediate referral. Diverticulitis (current or past): medical approval first, because colonic walls may be fragile. Acute gastroenteritis: avoid the area. Hernia: never try to reduce it; a strangulated hernia is an emergency. Reflux/ulcer: advise eating ≥2 h before treatment, avoid deep epigastric pressure. Long-standing constipation: caution, consider referral. [PTI-Dig]
- **Renal.** Kidney disease is a systemic contraindication unless supervised. Kidney stones: avoid, because increased blood flow raises intrarenal pressure. Acute bladder infection: defer until the GP clears it. [PTI-Urin]
- **Lymph.** Node clusters sit at the submandibular, axillary, cubital, inguinal and popliteal regions. Lymph moves by muscle pump, diaphragm suction, valves, peristalsis and gravity, so diaphragm work supports drainage. Enlarged tender nodes suggest infection: defer and refer. [PTI-Lymph]
- **Respiratory / breathing pattern disorder.** Over-breathing gives palpitations, atypical chest pain, cold extremities, dizziness, excessive sighing and yawning, and muscle pain in the occiput, neck, shoulders and interscapular area. Treat the diaphragm, scalenes, SCM and intercostals, then retrain breathing. Rule out cardiac causes first. [PTI-Resp]
- **Skin as an indicator.** Tight skin that won't slide suggests adhesion. Local tenderness suggests ischaemia or inflammation. Temperature differences suggest altered circulation or metabolism. Intermittent "dragging/juddering" on stroking may indicate trigger points. Avoid contagious lesions (warts, tinea, infected acne). [PTI-Skin]

### Neurophysiology useful for technique choice
- **MET/PNF:** the PTI neuro manual argues the post-contraction refractory period lets a muscle lengthen without protective spasm. Load the new range afterwards to keep it. [PTI-Neuro]
- **Proprioceptors:** muscle spindles (belly) detect stretch, sudden lengthening and excessive lengthening, and trigger the stretch reflex. Golgi tendon organs (musculotendinous junction) detect rising tension. Pacinian corpuscles detect pressure and vibration. Ruffini endings detect sustained heavy pressure. [PTI-Neuro]
- **Pain gate:** fast pressure/touch input inhibits slow deep-ache signals. This is the rationale for rhythmic compression near painful tissue. [PTI-Neuro]
- **Stress axis:** an unresolved stressor lasting more than ~15 min shifts to a cortisol response, which slows healing, disrupts sleep and depresses immunity. Parasympathetic-dominant treatment helps break the loop. [PTI-Neuro]
